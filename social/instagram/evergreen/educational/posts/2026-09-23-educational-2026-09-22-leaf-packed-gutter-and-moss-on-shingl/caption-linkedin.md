# Caption (linkedin)

Gutters packed with fir needles and leaves, moss lining up along the shingle joints. This is what we found on a recent fall inspection, and it's the kind of thing worth handling before the rains arrive.

When a gutter is full to the lip, rainwater has nowhere to go. It overflows at the eave and pools at the shingle edge. That's not a design flaw, it's a maintenance gap. And moss growing in the butt-edge joints holds moisture against the surface every time it rains, which in the Pacific Northwest is most of the year.

For property managers and building owners, fall is the right time to schedule gutter cleanouts and a quick roof surface assessment. Catching this now is a lot less expensive than tracing a moisture problem in February.
