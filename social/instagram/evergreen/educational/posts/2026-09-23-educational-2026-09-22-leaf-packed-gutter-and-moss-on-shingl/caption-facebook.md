# Caption (facebook)

Gutters don't fail. They just get ignored until the rain shows up and there's nowhere for the water to go.

This is what we see on a lot of roofs heading into fall. Leaves, fir needles, and debris packed level with the gutter lip. When rain hits a gutter that full, it doesn't drain. It overflows at the eave and sits right at the shingle edge, exactly where you don't want standing water.

And the moss growing along those shingle joints? Moss holds moisture against the surface. It's not just a cosmetic thing. It works against the roof every time it gets wet.

Neither of these is an emergency on its own. That's the point. Clear the gutters before the heavy rains hit, and if you've got moss lining up on the shingle edges, it's worth getting ahead of it before it settles in for winter.

A fall gutter cleanout and a quick look at the roof surface can save you a much bigger conversation in the spring.
