# Caption (facebook)

Most people know what shingles are. Fewer know why the hip of a roof needs something different.

A hip is where two roof faces meet and form an angled seam. Regular field shingles lie flat on each face, but they can't fold over that seam and seal it. That's where cap shingles come in. Each one bends over the hip and laps over the one below it, so the fasteners on each cap are covered by the next one up. That overlap is doing real work: keeping water out of the seam and protecting the nails underneath.

On this Oregon City roof we replaced, you can see the caps stepping down the hip. It's one of those details that's easy to walk past, but hips and ridges take more wind exposure than the flat field of a roof. After a windstorm, they're worth checking. If a cap is lifted, cracked, or missing, that seam is open.
