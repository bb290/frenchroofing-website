# Caption (linkedin)

Here's a roofing detail that doesn't get much attention until something goes wrong: hip cap shingles.

A hip is where two roof faces meet along an angled seam. Field shingles cover each face, but they can't bridge that joint. Cap shingles are designed to bend over the hip and lap one over the next, so each cap covers the fasteners of the one below it. It's a simple system: the caps shed water off the seam and keep the nails covered.

On a roof we replaced in Oregon City, you can see that lapped cap row running down the hip in the foreground. It's the kind of detail property managers and building owners should know about when they're doing post-storm checks. Hips and ridges are more exposed to wind than the main field of a roof. If a cap is lifted or missing after a windstorm, that's the spot to look at first.
