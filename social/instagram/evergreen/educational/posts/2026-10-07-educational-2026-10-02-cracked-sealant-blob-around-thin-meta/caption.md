# Caption

Sealant is not flashing. That's the short version, and it's worth knowing before you call any mound of caulk around a pipe a "fix."

We were on a roof in Gladstone recently and found this. A thin metal pipe coming up through the shingles, no flashing collar, no rubber boot. Just a troweled mound of gray mastic around the base. At some point that was somebody's solution, and for a while it probably held.

But sealant dries out. It goes through our PNW freeze-thaw cycles every winter, bakes in the summer sun, and eventually it cracks. You can see it in the photo: there's a visible gap opening up on the downhill side of the pipe, right where water wants to go.

A proper penetration gets a metal flashing or a rubber boot with a flange that tucks under the shingles on the uphill side. Water hits it and rides over, same as any other part of the roof. Caulk sits on top of the shingles with nothing to lap under. Once it splits, there's an open path for water to run down the outside of that pipe and into the roof deck.

If you've got a pipe poking through your roof, it's worth a look at what's sealing it. Not panic, just a look.
