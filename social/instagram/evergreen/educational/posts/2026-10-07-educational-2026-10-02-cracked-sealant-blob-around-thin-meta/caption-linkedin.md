# Caption (linkedin)

One thing we see fairly regularly on inspection visits: penetrations sealed with roofing mastic instead of proper metal flashing or a rubber boot. We spotted a good example recently on a roof in Gladstone.

The setup: a thin metal pipe through dark three-tab shingles, no flashing collar, no boot. Just a mound of gray sealant troweled around the base. On the downhill side, that sealant had cracked open, leaving a visible gap between the caulk and the pipe.

Here's why that matters for anyone managing a property. A correctly detailed penetration uses a flashing collar or rubber boot with a flange that tucks under the shingles on the uphill side. Water hits it and sheds over, the way the rest of the roof is designed to work. Sealant applied on top of the shingles has nothing to lap under. It dries, it cracks in the freeze-thaw, and once it splits there's an open path for water to travel down the outside of that pipe into the roof deck.

Caulk is a maintenance item at best, not a lasting solution for a roof penetration. If you manage properties and your roofs have had "sealed" penetrations over the years, it's worth having someone take a look. We hold CCB #203933 and have been doing this work in the Damascus area since 2014.
