# Caption (facebook)

Sealant is not flashing. That's the short version, and it's worth knowing before you call any mound of caulk around a pipe a "fix."

We were on a roof in Gladstone recently and spotted this: a thin metal pipe coming up through the shingles with no metal flashing collar and no rubber boot. Just a troweled mound of gray mastic around the base. At some point that was somebody's solution, and for a while it probably held.

But sealant dries out. It goes through PNW freeze-thaw cycles every winter, bakes in the summer, and eventually it cracks. In the photo you can see a gap opening up on the downhill side of the pipe, right where water wants to run.

A proper penetration gets a metal flashing or a rubber boot with a flange that tucks under the uphill shingles. Water sheds over it naturally. Caulk just sits on top with nothing to lap under. Once it splits, water has a clear path down the outside of that pipe into the roof deck.

If you've got pipes, vents, or anything else poking through your roof and you're not sure what's sealing them, it's worth a closer look before the rain season is fully underway.
