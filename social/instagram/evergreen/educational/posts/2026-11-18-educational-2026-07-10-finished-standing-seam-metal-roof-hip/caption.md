# Caption

Standing seam metal roofing is pretty simple once you see it up close. Each panel runs in one piece from the ridge all the way down to the eave. Where two panels meet, the edges lock together and form that raised seam. Water runs down the flat pan between the seams, with no sideways laps to work its way under.

Where two slopes meet at a hip, the panels are cut to meet along that line, and the joint gets its own metal cap in the same color over the top. Same idea at the ridge.

This one's a roof we finished in Boring this summer, tall firs and all.
