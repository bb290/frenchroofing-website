# Caption (linkedin)

Standing seam metal roofing tends to look complicated from the ground, but the logic behind it is straightforward once you're up close.

Each panel runs in one continuous piece from ridge to eave. Where neighboring panels meet, the edges interlock and form the raised seam. Water flows down the flat pan between those seams with no horizontal lap to work its way under.

At the hip, the panels from both slopes are cut to meet along the line, and that joint gets its own metal cap in a matching color. That's where the detail work shows.

This is a roof replacement we completed in Boring, OR this summer.
