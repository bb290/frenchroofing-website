# Caption (facebook)

Skylights are great until they're not. The most common call we get about them isn't the glass or the frame, it's the flashing.

A curb-mounted skylight sits above the roof deck on a raised frame. The shingles and step flashing have to wrap that curb cleanly, and the counter-flashing has to sit tight against it. When any of that gaps out, water finds it. Every time.

What makes skylight leaks tricky is the timing. The drip usually shows up days after the rain, once water has worked through insulation and framing. By then, you've forgotten which storm caused it.

If you've got a skylight that's 15 to 20 years old, or one that's fogged between the panes, it's worth having someone take a look at the flashing. Sometimes it's a reseal. Sometimes it's worth planning a replacement alongside the next roof. Either way, knowing is better than guessing.
