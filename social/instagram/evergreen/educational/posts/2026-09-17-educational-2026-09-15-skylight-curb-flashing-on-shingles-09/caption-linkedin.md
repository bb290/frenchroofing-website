# Caption (linkedin)

Skylights are a common source of roof calls, and it's almost never the glass that's the problem.

On a curb-mounted skylight, the flashing is doing most of the work. Step flashing, counter-flashing, and proper integration with the shingle courses all have to come together correctly. When one piece of that system ages out or was never installed tight to begin with, water works its way in quietly, often not showing up as a drip until days after the rain.

For property managers, that delay is the real headache. A tenant reports a stain, you're not sure if it's an old issue or a new one, and tracing it back takes time.

What we look for during an inspection: gaps at the curb, lifted counter-flashing, cracked sealant at the corners, and shingle wear around the frame. On a skylight that's pushing 15 to 20 years, we're also assessing whether a seal repair buys meaningful time or whether replacement makes more sense alongside the next roof cycle.
