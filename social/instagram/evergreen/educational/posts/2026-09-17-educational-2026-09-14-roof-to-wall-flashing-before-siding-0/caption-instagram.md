# Caption (instagram)

Flashing is one of those details that's easy to overlook because it's usually buried under siding. But this is where leaks start if it's done wrong.

This is roof-to-wall flashing running up the slope on an addition in Gresham. The siding hasn't gone on yet, so you can actually see what's happening underneath. Step flashing pieces overlap each other as they climb the wall, each one tucked under the course above. Done right, water has nowhere to go but down and away.

When we get a call about a leak along a wall, this is the first place we look. Nine times out of ten, the flashing was either skipped, too short, or installed after the siding instead of woven with it.

If you're adding onto your home, make sure your roofer and your siding crew are talking to each other before either one finishes their work. The handoff between those two trades is where this detail either gets done right or gets skipped.
