# Caption (linkedin)

Caulk is a maintenance item. Not a permanent fix. This parapet wall joint in Troutdale is a good reminder of that.

We were out for an inspection when we found this: metal cap meeting stucco, sealant cracked and pulled away, visible gaps. Nothing keeping water out. For a property manager or building owner, that's the kind of detail that's easy to miss on a walk-through but shows up fast once the rain gets in.

Parapet walls and wall-to-roof transitions are high-movement zones. Thermal cycling works on sealant joints constantly, and standard caulk has a lifespan. When that lifespan ends and the joint goes uninspected, you're one heavy rain away from a water intrusion problem that's genuinely difficult to trace.

The answer is simple: put sealant joints on a regular maintenance schedule. Every few years, inspect and reseal. It's a small line item that protects a much bigger investment.
