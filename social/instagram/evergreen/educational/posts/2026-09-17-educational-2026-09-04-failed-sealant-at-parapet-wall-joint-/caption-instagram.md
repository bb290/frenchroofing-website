# Caption (instagram)

Caulk is not a permanent fix. This is what happens when it gets treated like one.

This is a parapet wall joint in Troutdale. The metal cap meets the stucco, and the sealant that was supposed to keep water out has cracked, pulled away, and opened up visible gaps. At that point it's not sealing anything. It's just sitting there looking like it's doing a job it stopped doing a while ago.

Parapet caps and wall joints are high-movement spots. They expand and contract with every temperature swing. Standard caulk can't keep up with that forever. It needs to be inspected and redone on a regular schedule, not forgotten about until a leak shows up inside.

If your building has a parapet wall, put the sealant joints on your maintenance list. A fresh bead every few years costs a lot less than tracing a leak through a wet wall.
