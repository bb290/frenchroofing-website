# Caption (linkedin)

Wherever a roof meets a wall, you have a water problem waiting to happen, unless the flashing detail is right.

The roof surface alone won't keep water out at that transition. The fix is a metal flashing that lies flat on the roof and turns up against the wall, with the siding lapping over the top of the vertical leg. Water running down the wall hits the siding, drops onto the flashing, and drains out onto the roof. Nothing gets behind it.

That order, flashing first, siding over the top, is the whole thing. Reverse it and you've built a funnel pointing the wrong direction.

On this roof we replaced in Fairview, the flashing follows the wall base through an inside and an outside corner. Corners are where the detail either performs or fails, and on a low-slope roof there's very little pitch helping you out.
