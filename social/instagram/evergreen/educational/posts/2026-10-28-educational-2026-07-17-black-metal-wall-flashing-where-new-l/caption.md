# Caption

The spot where a roof meets a wall is one of the more reliable places for water to find its way in, if it isn't detailed right.

Roofing alone doesn't solve it. What actually keeps water out is a piece of metal flashing that lies flat on the roof surface and turns up against the wall. Then the siding laps down over the top of that vertical leg. So water running down the wall hits the siding, lands on the flashing, and flows out onto the roof, never behind it.

That sequencing matters. Flashing first, siding over the top. If it goes the other way, you've got an open invitation.

On this Fairview roof we replaced, the flashing follows the wall around an inside and an outside corner, so the whole jog is covered. Corners are where the detail work either holds or doesn't.

Next time you're looking at a low-slope roof that meets a wall, check that the siding comes down over the flashing, not the other way around. That one detail tells you a lot.
