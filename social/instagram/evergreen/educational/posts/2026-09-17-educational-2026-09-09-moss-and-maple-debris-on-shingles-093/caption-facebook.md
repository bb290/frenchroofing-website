# Caption (facebook)

Leaf litter and seed pods don't look like a big deal from the ground. Up close, they're holding moisture against your shingles around the clock.

We pulled up to an inspection recently and found exactly this: an older roof under a big overhanging maple, shingles packed with debris, and moss already filling in the gaps. The maple isn't the villain. It's just doing its thing. The problem is the stuff it drops never fully dries out. Wet debris against aging shingles speeds up granule loss, softens the mat underneath, and gives moss exactly the foothold it's looking for.

If you've got trees hanging over your roof, a debris clear-off once or twice a year makes a real difference. You don't need to take the tree down. You just need to keep the roof from staying wet.
