# Caption (linkedin)

We went out for an inspection recently and found an older roof under a big overhanging maple: shingles packed with leaf litter and seed pods, moss already working its way into the gaps.

This is one of the most common maintenance issues we see on PNW properties, and one of the most preventable. Debris that sits on shingles never fully dries out in our climate. That constant moisture accelerates granule loss, softens the shingle mat underneath, and gives moss a permanent foothold. By the time it's visible from the ground, the damage has usually been building for a couple of seasons.

For property managers and owners with tree-heavy lots, a debris clear-off once or twice a year is one of the lowest-cost ways to extend roof life. It's not glamorous maintenance, but it's the kind of thing that keeps a 15-year-old roof from becoming a replacement conversation ahead of schedule.
