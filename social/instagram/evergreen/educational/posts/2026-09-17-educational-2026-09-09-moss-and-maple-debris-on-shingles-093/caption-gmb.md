# Caption (gmb)

Roof maintenance in the Damascus area: debris and moss are a bigger deal than they look.

We stopped by an inspection recently and found an older roof with shingles packed in leaf litter and seed pods from an overhanging maple. Moss was already growing in the gaps. This is a classic Pacific Northwest maintenance situation: debris holds moisture, moisture feeds moss, and moss roots work their way into the shingle surface over time.

If you have trees hanging over your roof, a debris clear-off once or twice a year is one of the simplest ways to extend the life of your shingles. It's low-cost maintenance that can push off a much bigger expense.
