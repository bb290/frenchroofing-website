# Caption (facebook)

Most people can tell you what a roof looks like. Fewer know what it's actually doing up there.

The hip and ridge cap lines aren't just where the slopes meet. They're the last line of defense against wind uplift and water intrusion at the highest, most exposed points of the whole system. The shingles along those peaks take more abuse than anything else on the roof.

On an architectural shingle roof like this one, the ridge cap shingles are purpose-built. Thicker, pre-bent, and designed to flex through temperature swings without cracking. Standard field shingles cut and folded over the ridge is an older approach, and it tends to show its age faster.

That plumbing vent boot in the frame is worth paying attention to too. It's one of the more common spots we trace a slow leak back to during inspections.

Next time you're looking at a roof from the street, check where the slopes meet at the top. If the ridge cap looks wavy, thin, or lifted at the corners, it's worth a closer look before the rains hit.
