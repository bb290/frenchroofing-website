# Caption

Most people can tell you what a roof looks like. Fewer know what it's actually doing up there.

The hip and ridge cap lines on a roof aren't just where the slopes meet. They're the last line of defense against wind uplift and water intrusion at the highest, most exposed points of the whole system. The shingles that run along those peaks take more abuse than anything else on the roof.

On an architectural shingle roof like this one, the ridge cap shingles are purpose-built, thicker, pre-bent, and designed to flex with the roof deck through temperature swings without cracking. Standard field shingles cut and folded over the ridge is an older approach, and it shows its age faster.

That plumbing vent in the frame is worth watching too. The boot seal around it is one of the more common spots we trace a slow leak back to.

Next time you're looking at a roof from the street, check where the slopes meet at the top. If the ridge cap looks wavy, thin, or lifted at the corners, that's worth a closer look before the rainy season.
