# Caption (linkedin)

Three-tab shingles have a clear end-of-life look, and this photo is it. Tabs gone, the layer underneath exposed, and the remaining shingles usually just as worn, even if they haven't failed visibly yet.

Three-tab was the residential standard for decades. It's thinner than architectural shingles and takes a harder hit from Pacific Northwest conditions: sustained rain, moss pressure, and freeze-thaw cycles. By the time tabs start breaking off, the whole field is typically at or past the end of its service life.

For property managers, this is worth knowing: missing tabs on a three-tab roof usually mean you're past the repair window. Patching around gaps buys very little time and doesn't address the underlying material fatigue. A full replacement assessment is the more reliable path.
