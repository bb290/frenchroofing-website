# Caption

Three-tab shingles have a lifespan, and when they're done, they tell you pretty clearly.

This is what the end looks like. Tabs breaking off, the layer underneath exposed, nothing left between the weather and your roof deck. Three-tab was the standard for decades, but the material is thinner than architectural shingles and doesn't hold up as long in the Pacific Northwest. Moss pressure, freeze-thaw cycles, and year after year of rain all work against it faster than most folks expect.

When you start seeing missing tabs like this, it's not a repair situation. The shingles around the gaps are usually just as far gone, they just haven't let go yet.

If your roof is original three-tab and it's been up there 20-plus years, it's worth having someone take a look before the fall rains hit. A quick inspection tells you whether you've got time or whether this is the winter it finally gives.
