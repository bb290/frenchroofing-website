# Caption (gmb)

Chimney saddle flashing, what it is and why it matters for your roof.

That galvanized metal piece on the uphill side of a brick chimney is called a chimney saddle. Any chimney wider than about 30 inches needs one. Without it, water and debris collect right behind the stack, work into the flashing seam over time, and eventually reach the roof deck underneath.

The saddle's job is to steer water around both sides of the chimney before it can pond and cause problems. When it's in good shape and properly sealed, the chimney stops acting like a catch basin.

This is one of the details we check on every roof inspection in the Damascus area. A worn or missing saddle is a common reason chimney flashing fails earlier than expected, and it's often overlooked until a leak shows up.

If you have a brick chimney and haven't had the uphill side inspected recently, it's worth a look before the rainy season.
