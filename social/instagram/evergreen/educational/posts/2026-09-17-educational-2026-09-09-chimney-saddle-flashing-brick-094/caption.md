# Caption

See that metal saddle sitting on the uphill side of this brick chimney? That's called a chimney saddle, and it's one of the more important details on a roof.

Without it, any chimney wider than about 30 inches will collect debris and pond water right behind the stack. That standing water works into the flashing seam over time, and eventually into the roof deck underneath.

The saddle's job is simple: redirect water around both sides of the chimney before it ever gets a chance to sit. When it's installed right and sealed along the edges, the chimney stops being a catch basin.

We look at this detail on every inspection we do. A worn-out or missing saddle is one of the more common reasons a chimney flashing job fails earlier than it should.

If your chimney is more than two feet wide and you've never had someone look at what's happening uphill of it, it's worth a closer look before the rainy season hits.
