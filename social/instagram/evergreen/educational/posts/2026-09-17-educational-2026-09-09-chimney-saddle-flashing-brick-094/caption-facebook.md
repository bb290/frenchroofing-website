# Caption (facebook)

See that metal saddle on the uphill side of this brick chimney? That detail has a name: a chimney saddle, and it does more work than most homeowners realize.

Any chimney wider than about 30 inches will collect debris and pond water right behind the stack if there's nothing directing water away. Over time, that standing water finds its way into the flashing seam and eventually the roof deck underneath.

The saddle redirects water around both sides of the chimney before it can sit and cause trouble. When it's installed right and the edges are sealed, the chimney stops acting like a catch basin.

We check this detail on every inspection. A worn-out or missing saddle is one of the more common reasons a chimney flashing job fails sooner than it should. If your chimney is more than two feet wide and nobody's looked at what's happening on the uphill side lately, it's worth a closer look before the rains come back.
