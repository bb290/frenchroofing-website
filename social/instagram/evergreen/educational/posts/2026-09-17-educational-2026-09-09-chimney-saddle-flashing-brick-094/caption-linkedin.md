# Caption (linkedin)

That galvanized metal piece sitting on the uphill side of the brick chimney is called a chimney saddle, and it's one of the details we look at closely on every inspection.

Any chimney wider than about 30 inches will pond water and collect debris on the uphill side without one. That standing water eventually works into the flashing seam, and from there into the roof deck. The saddle's job is to redirect water around both sides of the chimney before it has a chance to sit.

We spotted this during a recent inspection. The saddle was present, which is good. It also tells us what to look for next: edge seals, condition of the step flashing below it, and whether any debris has bridged the gap.

For property managers overseeing older construction with brick chimneys, this is a detail worth confirming on your next roof assessment. A missing or deteriorated saddle tends to show up as a flashing leak that's easy to misread as something else.
