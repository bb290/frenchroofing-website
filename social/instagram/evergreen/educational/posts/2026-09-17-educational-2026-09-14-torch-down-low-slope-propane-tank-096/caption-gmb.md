# Caption (gmb)

Low-slope roof sections need a different material than the rest of the roof, and here's why.

This photo shows torch-down modified bitumen membrane going on over a shallow-pitch section. Shingles require slope to shed water fast enough to stay ahead of leaks. On a low-slope area, water moves too slowly and can back up under the tabs. Torch-down is a rubberized asphalt membrane that is bonded down with heat, creating a continuous layer with welded seams instead of overlapping tabs.

If your home or property has a section where the roof flattens out over a garage, addition, or covered porch, that section plays by different rules than the shingled slopes above it. It's worth knowing what material is up there and whether it's still doing its job.
