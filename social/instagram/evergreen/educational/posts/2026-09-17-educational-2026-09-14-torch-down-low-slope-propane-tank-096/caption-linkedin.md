# Caption (linkedin)

Low-slope roof sections and shingled slopes look like they belong together, but they don't follow the same rules.

The torch-down membrane going on in this photo (modified bitumen, a rubberized asphalt product) is the correct material for a shallow pitch. Shingles are designed to shed water fast. On a low-slope section, water moves slowly enough to back up under the tabs, which is where leaks start. Torch-down goes down as a continuous layer with heat-welded seams and no overlapping tabs for water to find.

For property managers and owners with mixed-slope roofs, this distinction matters at inspection time. The low-slope sections are often the first to show wear or failure, and they need a different eye than the pitched field above them.
