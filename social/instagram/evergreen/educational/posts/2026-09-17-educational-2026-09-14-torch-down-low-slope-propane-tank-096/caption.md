# Caption

Ever wonder why some parts of a roof get shingles and others get a membrane instead?

That rolled material going on here is torch-down, also called modified bitumen. It's a rubberized asphalt membrane, and on a low-slope section like this one, it's the right call. Shingles need pitch to shed water fast. On a shallow slope, water moves too slowly and can work its way under the tabs before it has a chance to run off. Torch-down creates a continuous, nearly seamless layer that doesn't rely on speed to keep water out.

The torch and propane heat the underside of the membrane as it unrolls, bonding it to the base layer underneath and welding the seams shut. No overlapping tabs for slow-moving water to find.

If your house has a section where the roof flattens out, over a garage, a bump-out addition, or a covered porch, that's the section worth paying attention to. It plays by different rules than the shingled slopes above it.
