# Caption (facebook)

Ever wonder why some parts of a roof get shingles and others get a membrane instead?

That rolled material going on in this photo is torch-down, also called modified bitumen. On a low-slope section like this one, it's the right material for the job. Shingles need pitch to shed water quickly. On a shallow slope, water moves too slowly and can work its way under the tabs. Torch-down creates a continuous, nearly seamless layer that doesn't depend on water speed to keep things dry.

The torch and propane heat the underside of the membrane as it unrolls, bonding it to the base layer underneath and welding the seams shut. No overlapping tabs for slow-moving water to find.

If your home has a section where the roof flattens out, over a garage, a bump-out addition, or a covered porch, that section plays by different rules than the shingled slopes above it. Worth knowing what you've got up there.
