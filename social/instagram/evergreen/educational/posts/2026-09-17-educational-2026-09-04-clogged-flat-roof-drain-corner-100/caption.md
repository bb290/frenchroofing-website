# Caption

Flat roof drains don't announce when they're in trouble. They just quietly fill up with dirt, leaves, and debris until water has nowhere to go.

We were out in Troutdale recently doing an inspection on a commercial flat roof. The drain in the corner was completely buried. You couldn't even see it. And those dark stains climbing the parapet walls? That's the waterline, showing exactly how high water had been sitting on that roof.

Standing water on a flat roof isn't just inconvenient. It adds serious weight, accelerates membrane breakdown, and finds every seam and penetration it can work into.

The fix here isn't complicated. A clogged drain is a maintenance problem, not always a replacement problem. Clear it, check the membrane around it, and make sure it's actually draining the slope it's supposed to.

If you've got a flat roof, commercial or residential, a quick look at your drains before the heavy fall rain hits is worth your time.
