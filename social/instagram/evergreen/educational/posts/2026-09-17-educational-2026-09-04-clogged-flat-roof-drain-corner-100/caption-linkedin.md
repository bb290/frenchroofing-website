# Caption (linkedin)

We were out in Troutdale recently doing a roof inspection on a commercial flat roof, and the drain in the corner was completely buried under dirt and debris. The staining up the parapet walls showed exactly how high water had been standing on that roof. You could read the problem before we even touched anything.

Standing water on a flat roof is one of those slow-moving issues that property managers don't always see coming. It adds load, breaks down the membrane faster than normal weathering, and eventually finds every seam and penetration it can exploit.

The important thing to understand: a clogged drain is usually a maintenance problem, not a replacement problem. Clear the drain, inspect the membrane condition around it, and verify the roof is still shedding to the right point. Catch it early and the fix is straightforward.

If you manage a commercial or multi-family building with a flat or low-slope roof in the Damascus or Troutdale area, this is exactly the kind of thing a pre-fall inspection catches before it becomes a claim.
