# Caption (facebook)

Flat roof drains don't announce when they're in trouble. They just quietly fill up until water has nowhere to go.

We were out in Troutdale recently doing an inspection on a commercial flat roof. The drain in the corner was completely buried under dirt and debris. And those dark stains climbing the parapet walls? That's the waterline, showing exactly how high water had been pooling on that roof.

Standing water on a flat roof adds serious weight, accelerates membrane breakdown, and finds every seam and penetration it can work into. It's one of those slow problems that looks minor until it isn't.

The good news is a clogged drain is usually a maintenance issue, not a replacement issue. Clear it, inspect the membrane around it, confirm the roof is draining the slope it's designed for, and you're in a much better spot heading into fall.

If you've got a flat or low-slope roof in the Damascus area, now's a good time to take a look before the heavy rain season kicks in.
