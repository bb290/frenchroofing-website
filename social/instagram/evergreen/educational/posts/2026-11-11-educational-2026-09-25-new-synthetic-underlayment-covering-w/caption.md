# Caption

Here's what a roof looks like in that in-between moment, tear-off done, shingles not on yet. That light-gray sheet covering the whole slope is synthetic underlayment, and it's doing more work than most people realize.

Underlayment is the layer that sits between your roof deck and your shingles. Think of it as a second line of defense. If water ever finds a way past a shingle, the underlayment is what stops it from getting into the wood underneath.

The way it goes on matters, too. Courses start at the gutter edge and work their way up the slope. Each row overlaps the one below it, so any water that gets under a shingle sheds down and off, not in. Same principle as shingles themselves, actually.

By the time the shingles go on, there's already a solid layer of protection underneath. Most homeowners never see this part of their roof. It's worth knowing it's there.
