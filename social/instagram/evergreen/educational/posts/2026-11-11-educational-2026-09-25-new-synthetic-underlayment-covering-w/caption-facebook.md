# Caption (facebook)

Here's what a roof looks like in that in-between moment, tear-off done, shingles not on yet. That light-gray sheet covering the whole slope is synthetic underlayment, and it does more work than most people realize.

Underlayment sits between the roof deck and the shingles. It's a second line of defense. If water ever finds a way past a shingle, the underlayment is what stops it from reaching the wood underneath.

The installation order matters, too. Courses start at the gutter edge and work their way up the slope. Each row overlaps the one below it so any water that gets under a shingle sheds downward and off, not inward. Same principle as the shingles themselves.

By the time the shingles go on, there's already a solid layer of protection built in. Most homeowners never see this part of their roof. It's worth knowing it's there.
