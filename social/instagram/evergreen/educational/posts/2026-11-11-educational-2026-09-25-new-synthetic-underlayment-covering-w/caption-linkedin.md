# Caption (linkedin)

Most people picture a roof as shingles. That's the visible part. But there's a layer underneath, between the deck and the shingles, called underlayment, and it's doing real work every time it rains.

This photo is from a roof replacement we completed recently. After tear-off, the whole slope gets covered in synthetic underlayment before a single shingle goes on. Each course starts at the gutter edge and laps over the one below it, so water sheds down and off rather than finding its way into the deck.

If that top layer ever gets compromised, the underlayment is what stands between the weather and the structure underneath. It's a detail that doesn't show once the job is done, but it matters every time there's a hard rain.
