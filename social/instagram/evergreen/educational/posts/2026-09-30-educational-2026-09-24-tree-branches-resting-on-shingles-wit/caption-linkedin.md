# Caption (linkedin)

Branches resting on a roof are easy to overlook. They're worth a second look before fall really sets in.

On a recent inspection in Gresham, we came across a large birch with several leafy branches lying directly on the shingles near the ridge. The photo tells the story: thin moss lines already tracking along the shingle joints, and a band of seed and catkin litter collecting at the eave.

Here's why it matters. Wind moves the branches, and those limbs scrub the granules off the shingles over time. Granules are the first line of defense against UV and rain. Lose enough of them and the shingles deteriorate faster. The leaf and seed litter compounds the problem, trapping moisture and keeping that part of the slope shaded, which is exactly what moss needs to take hold.

For property managers and owners keeping an eye on maintenance costs, "trim branches back off the roof" is one of the lowest-cost, highest-return items on the fall checklist. It's worth calling a tree service before the rainy season is in full swing.
