# Caption (instagram)

Branches touching your roof might not look like a big deal. But they are worth knowing about.

When the wind moves, those limbs scrub against the shingles and slowly wear off the granules. Granules are what protect the asphalt from UV and rain, so once they're gone, they're gone. At the same time, leaves, seeds, and catkin litter collect along the eave and in the gutters, hold moisture against the shingles, and give moss exactly the damp, shaded foothold it needs to get started.

We spotted this on an inspection in Gresham recently. Big birch, beautiful tree, branches resting right on the ridge end. Thin moss lines already starting along the shingle joints. A band of seed litter sitting at the eave. None of it catastrophic, but all of it avoidable.

Trimming branches back so they clear the roof is one of the simplest fall maintenance items there is. A good rule of thumb: if a branch can touch the roof, it's close enough to cause trouble. Have a tree service do it from a ladder or lift. No need to get on the roof yourself.
