# Caption (facebook)

Granules are the gritty coating on the surface of a shingle, and their whole job is to protect the asphalt underneath from UV and weather. When they wear away, the asphalt is exposed and the shingle starts breaking down.

This is what patchy granule loss looks like up close, from a roof we inspected in Gresham. Some tabs still have decent speckled coverage. Others have gone darker where the asphalt is showing through. And along the bottom edges of several tabs, the fiberglass mat is starting to show, pale and frayed. That fringe at the tab edge is a detail worth noticing.

The patchy, tab-by-tab pattern also matters here. When granule loss is spread unevenly across a whole slope rather than one scuffed spot, it usually means the shingles have been slowly losing coverage over time, not just taking a single hit.

It doesn't automatically mean a roof needs replacing, but it does mean it's worth having someone take a look. Knowing where you stand is a lot cheaper than finding out the hard way.
