# Caption (instagram)

Granules are the part of a shingle most people never think about. They're the gritty, sand-like coating on the surface, and their whole job is to shield the asphalt underneath from UV and weather. When they go, the asphalt is exposed, and the shingle starts breaking down fast.

This is what patchy granule loss looks like up close. Some tabs still have decent coverage. Others have gone dark where the asphalt is showing through. And along the bottom edges of several tabs, the fiberglass mat is starting to peek out, pale and frayed-looking. That fringe at the edge is worth paying attention to.

The patchy, tab-by-tab pattern matters too. This isn't one scuffed spot from a ladder or a branch. When granule loss is spread unevenly across the whole slope, it usually means the shingles have been slowly losing coverage over time rather than taking one hit.

We saw this on a roof in Gresham. It's not necessarily a "replace immediately" situation on its own, but it is the kind of thing worth getting eyes on sooner than later. A quick inspection tells you where you actually stand.
