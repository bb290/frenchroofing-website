# Caption (facebook)

One or two patches on a roof is completely normal. A good repair done at the right time can add real life to a roof. But when you start counting patches and lose track, that pattern is telling you something.

We were on a roof in Gladstone recently for an estimate. Scattered across the slope were a dozen or more replacement shingles and partial tabs, none of them matching the originals. Different shades, different textures, set in at different heights. The mismatches aren't the problem, replacement shingles never match an aged roof exactly, and that's fine. The pattern is the information.

What a pattern like this usually means is that the surrounding shingles were already brittle and losing granules each time a patch went in. Every repair addressed one spot while the field kept aging. At some point the honest conversation shifts from "what do we fix next" to "is the next repair money well spent, or is it time to look at the whole picture."

We're not here to push anyone toward a replacement they don't need. But if you're looking at a roof that's been patched more times than you can count, it's worth asking the question out loud before the next repair bill lands.
