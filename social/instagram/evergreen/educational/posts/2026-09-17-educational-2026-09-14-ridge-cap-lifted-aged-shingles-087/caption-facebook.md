# Caption (facebook)

Pulled up a ridge cap on a Gresham roof last week and this is what we found underneath. The shingles had aged past the point where the sealant strip was doing anything, and the ridge cap itself had lifted enough to let wind and water in from the very top of the roof.

Ridge caps take more abuse than any other part of a roof. They sit at the peak, catch wind from both sides, and bake in the sun more than a flat field shingle does. When they start to lift and the granules wear off the edges, that's not cosmetic, that's the roof opening up at its highest point.

What to look for from the ground: curling or raised shingles running along the peak, gaps between cap pieces, or heavier granule buildup in your gutters. If the ridge looks uneven or wavy, it's worth a closer look before the rain season hits.

A failing ridge cap isn't always a whole-roof situation. Sometimes it's a targeted repair. Either way, you want to know what you're dealing with before a windstorm makes the call for you.
