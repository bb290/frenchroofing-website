# Caption

Pulled up a ridge cap on a Gresham roof last week and this is what we found underneath. The shingles had aged past the point where the sealant strip was doing anything, and the ridge cap itself had lifted enough to let wind and water get in from the top.

Ridge caps take more abuse than any other part of the roof. They sit at the peak, catch wind from both sides, and bake in the sun more than a flat field shingle does. When they start to lift and the granules wear off the edges, that's not cosmetic, that's the roof opening up at its highest point.

What to look for from the ground: curling or raised ridge shingles running along the peak, any gaps between the cap pieces, or granule buildup in your gutters that's more textured than the rest. If the peak looks uneven or wavy, it's worth a closer look before the rain season hits.

A failing ridge cap usually isn't the whole roof. Sometimes it's a targeted repair. Either way, you want to know what you're dealing with before a windstorm makes the decision for you.
