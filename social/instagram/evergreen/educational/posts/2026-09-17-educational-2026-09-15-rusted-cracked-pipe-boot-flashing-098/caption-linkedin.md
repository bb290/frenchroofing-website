# Caption (linkedin)

Most homeowners don't know what a pipe boot is until one fails and water shows up inside.

Every plumbing vent that exits through a roof has a pipe boot: a metal base that laps over the shingles, and a rubber collar that seals around the pipe. It's a small detail, but it's one of the most common sources of roof leaks we find during inspections.

The rubber collar typically breaks down well before the surrounding shingles do. On a 12- to 15-year-old roof that's otherwise in solid shape, a split collar is a routine finding. Left alone, it lets water in. Caught early, it's a simple repair.

For property managers and GCs overseeing older buildings, pipe boots are worth including in any roof inspection checklist. A small repair today is a lot easier than tracing water damage through insulation and ceilings later.
