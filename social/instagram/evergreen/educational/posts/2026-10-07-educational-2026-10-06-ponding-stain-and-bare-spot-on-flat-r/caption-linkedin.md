# Caption (linkedin)

On a low-slope commercial roof, a dark tide-line stain with a bare granule-free circle in the center is one of the clearest signs of a drainage problem.

Here's what's happening: water that sits after rain (generally anything still standing around 48 hours after it stops is considered ponding) collects leaves and debris that hold moisture even longer. Over time, that standing water wears the protective granules off the modified bitumen cap sheet. Bare asphalt ages significantly faster in UV and weather than granule-covered asphalt.

The stain is the map. The fix is not coating over it; it's tracing why water collects there: a clogged drain or scupper, a sag in the deck, a low lap seam. Find the cause, then address it.
