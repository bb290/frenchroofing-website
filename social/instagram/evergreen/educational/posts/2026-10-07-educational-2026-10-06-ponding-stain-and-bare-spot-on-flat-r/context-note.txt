Teachable point a roofer would sign: a low-slope roof is designed to drain, not to hold water; where water sits after rain ('ponding', the usual rule of thumb is water still standing roughly 48 hours after it stops raining), the standing water and the leaves it collects wear the protective granules off the cap sheet and leave the asphalt bare and stained, and bare asphalt ages much faster in sun and weather; a dark ring like this is how you spot a low spot from the roof, and the fix is to find out why water is collecting there (clogged drain or scupper, a sag in the deck, a low seam) rather than to coat over the stain.
