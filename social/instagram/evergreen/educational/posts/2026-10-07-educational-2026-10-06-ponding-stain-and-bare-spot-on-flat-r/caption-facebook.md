# Caption (facebook)

See that dark ring with the bare spot in the center? That's a low spot on a low-slope roof telling on itself.

On a flat or low-slope roof, water that sits after rain is called ponding. The general rule of thumb is water still standing around 48 hours after rain stops. Ponding collects leaves and debris that hold moisture even longer, and over time it wears the protective granules right off the cap sheet. Once those granules are gone, the bare asphalt ages much faster in sun and weather.

The dark stain is the tide line. The bare circle is where the granules wore away. The fix isn't coating over the stain, it's finding out why water pools there: a clogged drain or scupper, a sag in the deck, a low lap seam. You address the cause, not the symptom.

If you manage a flat or low-slope roof and you're seeing marks like this after a rainy stretch, it's worth having someone walk it and trace where the water's going.
