# Caption (instagram)

See that dark ring and the bare spot in the middle? That's a low spot telling on itself.

On a low-slope roof, water that sits long enough after rain (the usual rule of thumb is anything still standing around 48 hours after it stops) is called ponding. And ponding does two things you don't want: it collects leaves and debris that hold moisture even longer, and it slowly wears the protective granules right off the cap sheet. Once the granules are gone, the asphalt underneath is exposed to the sun, and bare asphalt ages a lot faster than covered asphalt.

The stain is the tide line. The bare circle is where the granules lost the fight.

The fix isn't coating over the stain, it's figuring out why water is collecting there in the first place. Could be a clogged drain or scupper, a sag in the deck, or a low seam along a lap. You find the reason, then you address it. The stain is just the map.

If you manage a flat or low-slope roof and you've got marks like this after a rainy stretch, it's worth having someone walk it and trace where the water's going.
