# Caption (gmb)

Low-slope and flat roof maintenance, Damascus area commercial roofing.

On a commercial roof we looked at recently, we spotted a classic sign of a drainage issue: a dark tide-line ring with a bare, granule-free circle in the center of a modified bitumen cap sheet.

When water ponds on a flat roof (water still standing roughly 48 hours after rain stops), it collects leaves and debris that hold moisture longer, and gradually wears the protective granules off the surface. Once the granules are gone, bare asphalt is exposed to sun and weather and deteriorates much faster.

The stain marks where water sat. The bare spot marks where the granules lost the fight. Coating over it doesn't solve anything. The right move is finding why water collects there, whether that's a clogged drain or scupper, a sag in the deck, or a low lap seam, and addressing the cause.
